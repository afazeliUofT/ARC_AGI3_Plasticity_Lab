## ANSWER

**Option A.** Your simulation is right, the livelock is real, and the root error
is mine rather than a defect in your rule.

The throttle meters turn **wall-clock** as a stand-in for model consumption.
For a reasoning turn that is roughly fair. For a game-run it is plainly wrong:
most of a run's 1500-2000 s is engine stepping, backtests and exploration, and
at most 1200 s of it is model time under the cap already frozen in the config
hash. Charging the whole run against a 5000 s *model* ceiling made runs
unaffordable, and the deferral turns waiting for headroom refilled the meter
faster than age-out drained it. Options C and D would have papered over that
mis-accounting; B would have fixed the loop and kept the wrong price. Only A
fixes the thing that is actually wrong.

### What I have changed in `scripts/supervisor.py` (frozen file, my edit)

**A job runner.** Write `state/job_request.json`; the supervisor runs the
experiment as its own direct child **between turns**. No turn timeout over it,
no process killed at turn end, no credential in a model-controlled shell, and
its engine seconds are not charged as model spend.

```json
{"id": "g36-cd82-1",
 "runner": "run_experiment",
 "config": "configs/experiments/E300_ref.yaml",
 "game": "cd82",
 "wallclock_limit_s": 5400}
```

`runner` must be `run_experiment`. `config` must match
`configs/experiments/*.y(a)ml` and exist. `game` must match `[A-Za-z0-9_-]{1,16}`.
`wallclock_limit_s` is clamped to 3 hours. **The supervisor builds the argv
itself** - a job request is a request for an experiment, never a command line,
so it cannot become arbitrary execution with the credential present. A malformed
request is refused, recorded, and not retried.

The request file is consumed immediately. Results land in
`state/jobs/<id>/result.json`: `returncode`, `timed_out`, `wallclock_s`,
`model_seconds_charged`, `model_seconds_source`, and 8 KB tails of stdout and
stderr. Read it on your next turn. `state/jobs/` is gitignored; commit whatever
belongs in `artifacts/` yourself, as usual.

**Honest metering.** A job is charged the model seconds the run reports -
`model_wallclock_seconds_total` in the newest `results.json` written during the
job - and not its wall-clock. If a run reports no figure it is charged 1200 s,
the per-run cap, which is deliberately conservative. Keep writing that field;
it is now the thing that costs you.

**Hysteresis.** On reaching the ceiling the supervisor now sleeps until the
meter has drained to 60% of it (3000 s at the current 5000 s ceiling) instead of
resuming the moment one event ages out. That removes the sawtooth and, by
construction, makes your 3000 s launch condition reachable. You may keep that
rule or drop it: with jobs metered properly it is no longer load-bearing, and I
would rather you queue the job and let the supervisor decide when to run it.
